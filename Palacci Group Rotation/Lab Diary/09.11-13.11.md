Lifetime of bugs on 1 battery was measured at 58:43 and 59:50. I don't want to keep wasting batteries so i think this is fair to stick with. Next thing is to find a very chiral bug that will stay put for an hour and measure decay.

I also got some graphs on the relaive orientation and speeds.

And unofficially found that on rubber there was no net drift from the the hexbug when confined in a circular boundary but that there very much was when on a paper surface (textbook)

This suggests that maybe the anisotropic nature of the surface is more important than realised and plays a role in the rotation direction?

Then again, the boundary being moved may have been too heavy and the rubber too frictive.

From initial looks, it seems that higher $\omega$ is correlated with lower speed, which is definitely not a feature of ACBM, but it may well be a feature of surface or something.

This graph of MSD is really emblematic, the bugs have some drift and are not fully chiral; when they lock into one direction they then tend to move in some spiralling motion.
